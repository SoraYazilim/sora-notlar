When a production server goes down, the question that matters is not whether your data is safe — it is how long your business can operate without its systems. Data sitting intact in a backup repository is still an outage if the servers that use it are offline. Rebuilding failed hardware and restoring from backup can take hours or even days, and for many organizations that gap is the real disaster.

Acronis DRaaS (Disaster Recovery as a Service) closes that gap by keeping ready-to-run copies of production servers in the Acronis cloud. When hardware fails, ransomware strikes, or a natural disaster hits, the environment fails over to the cloud within minutes and operations continue. The RunVM engine converts backup images directly into runnable virtual machines, which is how RPO and RTO targets under 15 minutes become achievable. Failover across Acronis Cloud, Microsoft Azure, or a hybrid setup is managed from a single console.

Automation, testing, and ransomware resilience

Complex environments cannot be brought up in random order. Runbooks define dependency sequences — database server first, then the application server, then the web tier — and automate the failover steps. A site-to-site VPN keeps the local network and the cloud securely connected, so partial failover works: systems moved to the cloud keep talking to those that stayed on premises. Failback is part of the plan too, returning workloads to the local environment without data loss once the incident is resolved.

Against ransomware, the decisive capability is failing over to a clean, unencrypted recovery point taken before the attack, which prevents reinfection. Combined with Acronis immutable backup options, the backups themselves stay protected. And because an untested backup is not a backup, test failover runs in an isolated environment and validates the whole plan regularly without touching production.

A sound DRaaS design starts with business impact analysis: systems are tiered by criticality, measurable RPO/RTO targets are set per tier, and runbooks are kept current as the infrastructure changes. The cloud-based model also turns what used to be a secondary data center investment into a monthly subscription, putting enterprise-class business continuity within reach of small and mid-sized organizations.
