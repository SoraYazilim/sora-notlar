Ask a room full of network engineers which next-generation firewall is "the best" and you will get several confident, contradictory answers. That is not because most of them are wrong — it is because the question is incomplete. FortiGate, Palo Alto, Check Point, and Sophos are all strong NGFW platforms that optimize for different priorities, and a serious evaluation starts by accepting that there is no universal winner.

The stakes keep rising. Gartner forecasts worldwide end-user spending on network security to grow from $21.3 billion in 2024 to $25.8 billion in 2026, within an information security market reaching $213 billion in 2025. Meanwhile, the Verizon 2025 Data Breach Investigations Report found that exploitation of vulnerabilities as an initial attack vector grew 34% year-over-year and now factors into 20% of breaches, with attackers focusing heavily on perimeter devices and VPNs. The firewall you choose is itself part of your attack surface.

Four platforms, four centers of gravity

FortiGate combines ASIC-based hardware acceleration for strong price/performance with integrated SD-WAN and a broad model range; Fortinet holds more than 50% of the global unit market and is a 2025 Gartner Hybrid Mesh Firewall Leader, which translates into abundant training resources, a wide partner network, and a mature ecosystem. Palo Alto is positioned on application visibility and advanced threat prevention, Check Point on granular policy design and mature central management for complex multi-site rule sets, and Sophos on ease of use and endpoint-firewall synchronized security, particularly at SMB scale.

An objective comparison weighs six criteria together: price/performance, management and ease of use, threat-prevention capability, SD-WAN and integration, ecosystem, and total cost of ownership (TCO). Availability belongs in the calculation as well: unplanned downtime costs Global 2000 companies $600 billion a year — a 50% increase in two years, averaging $15,000 per minute — which is the business case for firewall high availability and clustering.

The method is straightforward: clarify your priorities, shortlist two or three candidates, measure Threat Protection performance in a proof of concept close to real traffic, weigh the management experience against your team's expertise, and calculate multi-year TCO including security subscriptions and renewals. Testing every shortlisted candidate against the same scenarios keeps the results comparable; avoid one-dimensional, price-only comparisons.
