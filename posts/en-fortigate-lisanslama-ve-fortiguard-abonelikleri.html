When an organization budgets for a next-generation firewall, most of the attention goes to picking the hardware model. Yet the box itself is only half the story. Without a FortiGuard subscription, a FortiGate still performs basic firewalling, NAT, and VPN — but the protections that actually track today's threats, such as antivirus, IPS, and web filtering, depend on time-based FortiGuard services that have to be planned, budgeted, and renewed like any operational commitment.

The licensing model splits into three clean parts: the hardware you buy once (or a FortiGate-VM licensed by vCPU count and subscription term, available as BYOL or as PAYG in cloud marketplaces); FortiGuard subscriptions that continuously update antivirus and IPS signatures, web and DNS filtering categories, application signatures, and botnet reputation data; and FortiCare support covering hardware replacement (RMA), technical support, and firmware access. Security subscriptions and support are usually sold under the same bundle, which is why device selection and license planning belong in the same conversation.

Bundles range from basic, IPS-focused options to UTP — typically antivirus, IPS, web and DNS filtering, and application control with support — and Enterprise/ATP tiers that add further services on top. The deciding factor is which security profiles you will actually enable: an overly broad bundle inflates cost, while a narrow one leaves protection gaps.

Renewal Is a Security Decision, Not Paperwork

Expiry is a quiet failure mode. FortiGuard updates stop, basic protection may coast on existing signatures for a while, and some services are fully disabled — all without any visible outage. The threat landscape raises the stakes: exploitation of vulnerabilities as an initial attack vector grew 34% year over year and now factors into 20% of breaches, with attackers focusing heavily on perimeter devices and VPNs. Renew before expiry, and if you run many devices, track renewal dates centrally with FortiManager or inventory tracking so expiry never comes as a surprise.
